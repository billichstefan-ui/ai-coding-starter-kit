## Short 1 — "Left on read" (the relatable entry)
- **Hook (0–2s):** "The message says *Read*. Then nothing. And your chest tightens — over silence."
- **Script (~40s):**
  > You told yourself that's stupid. It's just a text. But your body didn't listen. Here's why:
  > when researchers put people in a brain scanner and secretly excluded them from a simple game,
  > the brain lit up the *same* region it uses for physical pain. No insult. No touch. Just being
  > left out — and the alarm fired. Because for most of human history, being cast out of the group
  > meant death. Your brain never got the memo that it doesn't anymore. So no — you're not too
  > sensitive. You're running very old software.
- **Visual:** macro phone read-receipt → silhouette w/ red sternum glow → brain node ignites → savanna fire.
- **Caption/title:** `Being left on read isn't "nothing" 🧠` · **CTA:** "Full breakdown on the channel → Hidden Rush"

## Short 2 — "A painkiller for a broken heart" (the shock fact)
- **Hook (0–2s):** "A painkiller in your bathroom can dull a broken heart. That's a real study."
- **Script (~45s):**
  > In 2010, researchers gave people either a daily dose of acetaminophen — the stuff in Tylenol —
  > or a sugar pill, for three weeks. Nobody knew which. The group on the painkiller reported
  > *fewer hurt feelings*. And their brains showed a quieter response to social rejection, in the
  > exact region tied to physical pain. A physical painkiller… reaching into emotional pain. This
  > isn't advice — don't treat loneliness with pills. But as evidence it's wild: it means social
  > pain and physical pain aren't just similar. They share the wiring.
- **Visual:** single pill w/ red crack → two pills (one dissolves) → brain node cooling red→blue.
- **Caption/title:** `Tylenol… for hurt feelings? 💊` · **CTA:** "The full story → Hidden Rush"

## Short 3 — "The smoke detector" (the reframe / most shareable)
- **Hook (0–2s):** "Being ignored hurts because your brain is a smoke detector that's stuck."
- **Script (~38s):**
  > The pain of being left out is real — but the *threat* it's reporting usually isn't. Being left
  > off a text won't get you eaten. Being talked over won't leave you to freeze in the dark. Your
  > brain is running a survival alarm calibrated for a world that's gone — still screaming at the
  > smell of toast. Here's the useful part: you can't switch the alarm off, but you can learn its
  > voice. *That's the old alarm. Real pain. Outdated threat.* And a feeling you can name is a
  > feeling that stops running the room.
- **Visual:** blinking smoke detector → toast smoke → red glow cooling to blue → steady silhouette.
- **Caption/title:** `Why "just ignore it" never works` · **CTA:** "Learn to read the pattern → Hidden Rush"
